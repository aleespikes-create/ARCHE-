Why nothing has held

The strategy was the problem.

You counted. You walked. You cut carbs, then cut them again. Maybe you even got the shot from an app that never asked your name. Somewhere in there you started to suspect the problem was you. The standard approach

Measure first, then treat what you find

  • A comprehensive nutritional and metabolic panel before your protocol is built.

The mechanism

Three questions your program
should be able to answer

Not "how much did you lose this week." These:

01

What is your metabolism actually doing?

A first-morning urine sample is analyzed for organic acids, amino acids, and oxidative stress markers — the byproducts your cells leave behind. It's a readout of how you're producing energy, processing protein, and handling oxidative load right now.

Why it matters: two people the same height and weight can have completely different metabolic pictures. Treating them identically is how one of them ends up in the "it didn't work for me" pile.

02

What is the weight actually made of?

An in-clinic body composition scan separates fat mass from lean mass and gives you a starting line that a scale cannot. Every follow-up scan is measured against it.

Why it matters: rapid weight loss can take muscle with it. If all you track is total pounds, you can't tell a good week from a bad one — and muscle is what keeps the weight off later.

03

Who is choosing your dose, and what are they looking at?

At ARCHE, that's Dr. Muvvala — an osteopathic physician with training in integrative medicine — sitting across from you with your panel and your scan in front of him.

Why it matters: a starting dose picked from an intake form is an average applied to an individual. Titration decisions get better when there's data behind them.
